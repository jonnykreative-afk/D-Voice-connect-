# PUBLIC LANDING — Design Brief

**Product:** D'voice
**Surface:** Public landing page (signed-out Home)
**Platform:** Mobile-first web
**Status:** Ready for design

---

## 1. Purpose

Convert a visitor who does not know D'voice into someone who either:
- downloads a resource immediately, or
- signs up.

Everything on the page serves one of those two goals.

This is the **one surface where the founder is allowed to be central.**
Everywhere else in the signed-in product, founder presence is a quiet
link. Here, it is the framing.

---

## 2. Order of persuasion

1. **Founder framing** — brief, at the top. Earns attention.
2. **Resources** — immediately after. Earns trust.
3. **CTA** — persistent. Converts.

A landing page that opens with the founder and never shows a resource is
a vanity page. A landing page that opens with resources and buries the
founder wastes the product's one distinct asset.

---

## 3. Sections, in order

### 3.1 Founder framing
Short. Who founded D'voice, in a sentence or two. Not a biography.
Signals that a real person stands behind it.

### 3.2 What D'voice is
One clear statement: an educational resource platform for students.
Concrete, not sloganeering.

### 3.3 Resource proof
Show real resources from the public catalogue — a small strip or grid.
This is what makes the claim credible. Tapping one opens its details
page (public, no sign-in).

### 3.4 Primary CTA
- **Browse resources** → public Explore. This is the lowest-friction
  action and must be the most prominent.
- **Sign up** → auth. Present, but secondary to browsing. Do not gate
  discovery behind registration.

### 3.5 Community
WhatsApp community link. Optional to join. Never a condition for
accessing resources.

### 3.6 Footer
Sign-in link for returning users. Any legal/contact links.

---

## 4. What the landing page must NOT do

- Gate resource access behind sign-up.
- Open with a founder biography.
- Read as a personal campaign page rather than a product page.
- Hide the resources.
- Require sign-in to browse or download.

---

## 5. Relationship to signed-in Home

These are **two different pages** that may share a URL:

- **Signed-out:** founder-framed landing page (this brief).
- **Signed-in:** routing surface (separate brief).

They are not one page with a toggle. Do not design them as variants of
each other.

---

## 6. Relationship to public Explore

Signed-out users reach **the same Explore** as signed-in users. No
separate signed-out catalogue, no gated preview, no blurred content.
Differences are minor and handled in Explore's own brief. Navigation
between landing and Explore must be obvious and one tap.

---

## 7. States

- **Loading:** section-level skeletons.
- **Empty catalogue:** landing still works — founder framing, CTA, and
  community stand without resource proof. Do not fake resources.
- **Error:** affected section degrades quietly; rest of page usable.

---

## 8. Accessibility floor

- Tap targets ≥ 44px.
- Real heading order.
- CTA is a real button/link, keyboard reachable.
- Community link announced clearly as optional.

---

## 9. Larger screens

Founder framing and resource proof may sit side by side. CTA remains
above the fold. Content and order unchanged.

---

## 10. Visual direction

- Blueish, modern. Not the generic AI-app blue.
- One secondary colour carrying semantic load.
- No glow.
- Warmer and more expressive than the signed-in product. This page is
  allowed personality the dashboard is not.

---

## 11. Out of scope

- News.
- Full founder biography or About page (linked, not embedded).
- Testimonials.
- Blog.
- Any gated content.

---

## 12. Decisions already made (do not relitigate)

- Signed-out Home is a landing page, founder-focused.
- Signed-out Explore is the same Explore, minor differences only.
- Browsing is the primary CTA. Sign-up is secondary.
- Resources are never gated.
- This is a distinct page from signed-in Home.
